I came to machine learning through automation engineering, which is probably why I care more about what a model does on a bad day than on a good one.

Je suis venue à l'apprentissage automatique par le génie de l'automatisation, ce qui explique sans doute pourquoi je m'intéresse davantage à ce qu'un modèle fait un mauvais jour qu'un bon.

I studied electrical and automation engineering in Tunisia, came to Canada on a Mitacs Globalink internship at McGill, and stayed for a master's at Concordia. The research was on domain adaptation. The year after it was on a production line. Those two things turned out to be the same subject.
